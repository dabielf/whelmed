# Product

## Register

product

## Users

The first user is François. He may open Whelmed with little energy or a fried brain, on either a phone or a computer, and needs the interface to remain easy to consult and update in that state.

## Product Purpose

Whelmed keeps the user's Values and Goals close at hand. It reduces the effort needed to consult them, choose an action, or record one. It helps the user act on each Active Value daily and judge for themselves whether their actions also support their visible Goals, without calculating or linking Goal alignment.

## Brand Personality

Calm, gentle, clear. Whelmed feels like a quiet facilitator and reminder, not an authority judging the user. The displayed wordmark is **Whelmed.**, including the final period.

## Anti-references

Whelmed must not feel clinical, childish, gamified, or driven by productivity pressure. It must not turn Values into scores, streaks, moral judgments, or an overall complete-or-incomplete day.

## Design Principles

- Keep Values and chosen Goals easy to consult from the main flow.
- Make adding or completing a Daily Action take very little effort.
- Keep Daily Actions focused on today; Goals hold future direction so Whelmed does not become a task planner.
- Accept that useful actions may go unlogged; Whelmed does not ask the person to repair past Daily Logs.
- Start the new-action form with its Done switch on; the person can turn it off when planning an action for later today.
- Keep one Value as the normal focus for each Daily Action; extra Values are available but never promoted as a shortcut.
- Include known shortcuts that reduce the effort to read, choose, or enter information in the first build; visual polish may wait.
- Show progress as useful information, never pressure or praise.
- Keep choices visible and language plain when the user's capacity is low.
- Work comfortably on both phone and computer.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Use readable text, strong contrast, visible keyboard focus, full keyboard access, and reduced-motion support. Never rely on color alone for meaning. Keep copy short and avoid avoidable cognitive load.
